# Motion reference playbook

These are the user's primary taste references, selected after the earlier showreel fell short. The target is authored motion: clear visual ideas, developed artwork, spatial continuity, varied timing, and a designed beginning and ending. The latest brief always wins. Choose the relevant clips; do not combine all 22 in every project.

## Before designing

1. Use the [library](README.md) to choose one primary reference and up to two supporting references for the actual brief. Read their study cards and reopen the relevant source interval and consecutive frames. Search tags when the user describes a technique rather than a title.
2. Put `Reference intent` in the project blueprint/storyboard: IDs, source time ranges, the specific behaviour to adapt, what must remain recognisable across the transition, and what will be original to this project. Include opening, acceleration, reading intervals and ending. A style name or palette alone is insufficient.
3. Preserve existing user answers and music exclusions. Inventory newly supplied music before sound design; the user explicitly excluded **Ivan is Loading** and accepted **The Golden Hour Rise** for the showreel. Those are project directions, not instructions to use that track for every future brief.
4. Follow the master's storyboard preference rule for new building work. This reference study itself does not start a new production or require a questionnaire.

## What makes the movement work

| Principle | Evidence to reopen | How to apply it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A transition changes the current object into the next idea | [REF-02](studies/REF-02.md) 0.4–1.0; [REF-21](studies/REF-21.md) 1.6–2.2 | Keep an anchor such as the ball centre or card centre; change role, shape and contents on overlapping timings. |
| A brand motif does useful work | [REF-22](studies/REF-22.md) 3.6–4.2 and 34–36 | Logo books become progress bars and return to the logo. Invent a similarly functional motif for our product. |
| One asset survives several contexts | [REF-09](studies/REF-09.md) 5.7–26.5 | Gallery image → edited image → phone → print. Maintain its crop/identity and motivate each scale change. |
| Camera travel changes what the viewer understands | [REF-01](studies/REF-01.md) 22–59; [REF-17](studies/REF-17.md) 7–10 | Establish the whole, approach a detail, explain it, then restore orientation. Plan foreground occlusion and focus recovery. |
| Timing has contrast | [REF-14](studies/REF-14.md) 104–121; [REF-15](studies/REF-15.md) 8–14.5 | Quiet setup → short burst → resolved reading hold. Do not force the same duration and spring onto every word. |

## Three concrete transition recipes

**Container morph, adapted from REF-21.** Identify the shared centre and silhouette. Start height/radius changes first, retain the artwork tile, clear obsolete content, then resolve the new text and controls. The enlarged f98–133 study spans 0.583s at native 60fps; it is an observation window, not a mandatory duration. Preserve continuity when scrubbing in both directions.

**Staggered curtain, adapted from REF-20.** Bars rise at different offsets; every pixel of the old scene is covered before the reveal. Continue the bars upward rather than reversing them; start the incoming count behind the clearing curtain. The f540–575 strip shows the cover and reveal within 0.583s. Design the safe full-coverage interval explicitly.

**Button iris, adapted from REF-09.** Complete the action and show its checkmark. Expand the actual button about its own centre until it occludes the stage, then reveal the physical result. At f1303 / 21.717s expansion is visible; by f1312 / 21.867s the old corners are covered. This is a useful very short transition embedded in a much slower product story.

These are implementation interpretations, not recovered easing curves. Rebuild with deterministic frame-based animation on the existing engine, convert source seconds to the new project's integer frame rate, and judge the adapted result at playback speed.

## Sound lessons supported by evidence

All 18 audio-bearing files have local earshot timeline reports; REF-07/11/12/16 contain no audio stream. See [measured comparisons](AUDIO_MEASUREMENTS.json). The files are already mixed, so music/SFX cannot reliably be separated by inspecting an onset tag. No human listening pass is claimed.

- REF-14 has 960 detected events and weak pulse clarity 0.165: a dense onset timeline does not establish one effect per letter or a dependable beat grid.
- REF-15 has 139 detected events in about 14.5s and +0.2dBTP decoded peak; REF-19 measures +1.0dBTP and a clipped-sample run around 19.70s. Treat these as rhythm references and measure our production mix independently.
- REF-01 measures -38.2LUFS with no dependable pulse (clarity 0.079). Cinematic motion does not imply a loud continuous beat.
- The nearest event to a selected visual frame is recorded with a signed millisecond offset on each study card. This is evidence to investigate, not a universal sync verdict. Model tags and auto-detected cuts can be wrong; morphs need visual review.

For production, inventory and choose the user's music first, map meaningful motion accents to it, use Real SFX for appropriate recorded-effects placement and earshot for fit/trims/mix measurement. Source gaps through Openverse or generate appropriate missing accents within the brief. Do not copy reference audio or impose a sound on every sub-element merely to inflate cue count.

## Review against the references

Compare an early proof of the opening, one difficult transition and the ending with the selected reference intervals before building the full film. Review moving playback plus enlarged boundary frames, not just a flattering contact sheet. Record a specific gap and fix it: missing intermediate state, wrong centre/pivot, unreadable data, weak material, flat depth, identical timing, or abrupt ending. Retain the master's applicable production review rounds and validation requirements.

The user's selected references take priority over generic aesthetic defaults when a deliberate reference-backed choice needs multiple accents, a stable camera, a reading hold, a functional split layout, a wash or a hard snap. REF-12 and REF-16 are useful quiet process/hierarchy references; they are not a reason to lower the energy of an explicitly dynamic reel. REF-19's black tail is not an intended ending to emulate.
